# Chapter 2: AI Fundamentals for Physical Systems

## Overview
This chapter delves into the core principles of artificial intelligence that are particularly relevant to physical systems and robotics. We explore machine learning algorithms, computer vision, natural language processing, and control theory as they apply to embodied AI systems. The chapter emphasizes the unique challenges of implementing AI in real-time physical environments.

## Sections
### Section 1: Machine Learning in Physical Systems
Machine learning forms the backbone of modern AI systems, but its application in physical systems presents unique challenges. This section covers supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning paradigms with focus on their application to robotic systems. We discuss how traditional ML approaches must be adapted for real-time constraints, sensor fusion, and safety-critical operations. Special attention is given to deep learning architectures suitable for perception and control tasks in robotics, including convolutional neural networks for vision processing and recurrent networks for temporal sequence modeling.

### Section 2: Computer Vision and Sensory Perception
Robots operating in the physical world rely heavily on computer vision and sensory perception to understand their environment. This section explores techniques for object recognition, scene understanding, depth estimation, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). We examine how multiple sensors (cameras, LiDAR, IMUs) must be integrated to create a coherent understanding of the physical space. The discussion includes challenges such as lighting variations, occlusions, and real-time processing requirements that differentiate robotic vision from traditional computer vision applications.

### Section 3: Control Theory and Decision Making
Effective physical AI systems require sophisticated control mechanisms that bridge high-level goals with low-level actuator commands. This section covers classical control theory, adaptive control, and modern approaches to robot control including model predictive control and learning-based control. We explore how decision-making algorithms must account for uncertainty, delays, and the continuous nature of physical environments. The chapter also addresses the integration of symbolic AI approaches with subsymbolic learning methods in embodied systems.
